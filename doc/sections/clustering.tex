\subsection{Centroid-based Clustering}
\label{centroid_clustering}
In analyzing the dataset using centroid-based methods, we experimented with three different algorithms: K-Means, Bisecting K-Means, and X-Means.
For each experiment, we followed the same steps:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Choosing the attributes: we experimented by using both all numeric features (vide Table \ref{tab:dataset_numeric}), log-transformed if necessary as detailed in Subsection \ref{numeric_vars_analysis}, and then by only using a selection of them. In fact, in light of our findings in Subsection \ref{var_correlation_elimination}, we dropped \textit{totalImages}, \textit{numRegions} and \textit{totalCredits}, since they are all moderately correlated with \textit{numVotes}. Using this selection of features has yielded better performances.
    \item Scaling the dataset: we used min-max normalization, since it lead to better Silhouette scores in comparison to StandardScaler and RobustScaler.
    \item Selecting the best value for \textit{k} (except for X-Means): we used both the Elbow method and the Silhouette method as heuristics to determine the appropriate number of clusters. To do so, we iteratively run the algorithm for \textit{k} in the range [2,50], plotting the SSE and Silhouette score for each \textit{k} and comparing the two curves to find the best trade-off. An example is reported in Figure \ref{fig:elbow_kmeans1.png}.
    \item Fitting the algorithm and evaluating the resulting clustering in terms of cohesion and separation by calculating the SSE, Silhouette score, and correlation between the distance matrix of the dataset and the ideal similarity matrix.
    \item Visual cluster exploration: cluster visualization through PCA (and t-SNE for selected experiments), parallel coordinates plot of the centroids, and silhouette plots for each cluster.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{K-Means}
For the first experiment using all numeric features, we select k=8 as a trade-off between SSE and Silhouette score, by analyzing the plots in Figure \ref{fig:elbow_kmeans1.png}.
The resulting SSE and Silhouette score are 1843.054 and 0.333, respectively, while the correlation is -0.616.

\begin{figure}
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{../results/images/elbow_kmeans1.png}
    \caption{SSE and Silhouette score for choosing \textit{k} (K-Means, all feats.)}
    \label{fig:elbow_kmeans1.png}
\end{figure}

The clusters' composition is reported in Table \ref{tab:clusters_kmeans1}: in terms of population, we can distinguish Cluster 2 as the largest cluster, and then three groups, with Clusters 5, 6 and 7 having slightly less than 1000 records, Clusters 0 and 3 having less than 1500, and Clusters 1 and 4 having between 3000 and 3500 records.
The first thing that emerges from the PCA visualization and the parallel coordinates graph is that the clusters separate points mostly based on \textit{numGenres} (which only has three discrete values, 1, 2, or 3). \textit{criticReviewsRatio} also leads to interesting centroid separation. Cluster 7 is characterized by a significantly lower value for \textit{startYear}, meaning that it clusters older titles; its centroid is also separated by a lower value for \textit{totalCredits} and \textit{logRuntime}, though not as dramatically.


For the second experiment, using the selection of features, we also select k=8. All evaluation metrics improve: the resulting SSE is 989.711, the Silhouette score is 0.444, and the correlation is -0.656. Figure \ref{fig:SSE_eval_kmeans2.png} shows that our clustering's SSE is significantly lower than the SSE found in the clustering of 500 randomly generated datasets, which suggests that the clusters found by the algorithm in our dataset are meaningful and not a product of chance.

Looking at the silhouette plots in Figure \ref{fig:silhouette_kmeans2.png}, we can say that Clusters 6, 7, and 0 are particularly well-separated, with a significant portion of their data points extending beyond the average Silhouette score, whereas Clusters 1, 2 and 3 seem to be less well-defined.

\subsubsection{Bisecting K-Means}
For the first experiment, using all numeric features, we select k=6, even though the elbow for the SSE is k=12, because after that peak the Silhouette score drops significantly. The evaluation metrics are overall disappointing: the SSE is 2142.045, the Silhouette score is 0.337, and the correlation is -0.634.

The cluster's composition is summarized in Table \ref{tab:clusters_bkmeans1}: Cluster 3 is the most populated cluster with over 5000 records, followed by Cluster 0 and 4 having both more than 3600, whereas the remaining clusters have between 1197 and 1435 titles. Similarly to before, the clusters' centroids are mainly separated w.r.t. \textit{numGenres} and \textit{criticReviewsRatio}; Cluster 2 distinguishes itself with particularly high values for \textit{numVotes}, \textit{totalImages}, \textit{totalCredits} and \textit{numRegions}.


The experiment using fewer features, for which we selected k=6, yields better results, with a SSE equal to 1245.326, a Silhouette score equal to 0.436, and the best correlation among all experiments (-0.672). We have two larger clusters, 0 and 5, while the smallest has 995 features (Table \ref{tab:clusters_bkmeans2}). Once again, the centroids separate mainly based on \textit{numGenres} and \textit{criticReviewsRatio}; Cluster 3 has a particularly high value for \textit{numVotes} w.r.t. the others.

\subsubsection{X-Means}
Since using fewer features has yielded better results thus far, we employed the selected subset of numerical features. In the first experiment, we did not set a limit to the execution of the algorithm; the result was a clustering with 20 clusters, which of course led to a lower SSE (534.554), but a less satisfactory Silhouette score (0.313) and the lowest correlation observed among the experiments (-0.477). Since 20 clusters are difficult to analyze, in the following experiments we set k=8 and k=6. We report the results for k=8, which has yielded overall satisfactory results in comparison to most of the experiments, with a SSE equal to 1032.925, a Silhouette score of 0.436, and a correlation of -0.652. The cluster's composition (Table \ref{tab:clusters_xmeans}) echoes the ones seen for the previous algorithms, with one larger cluster, a couple of medium-large clusters, and four clusters ranging from 681 to 957 records. The clusters' centroids are, once again, mostly separated based on \textit{numGenres} and \textit{criticReviewsRatio}.

In order to evaluate the results, we first visualized the distribution of the clusters against known categorical features (those in Table \ref{tab:dataset_categorical}, except for \textit{originalTitle} because of its uninformativeness, and \textit{rating} because it is redundant w.r.t. \textit{ratingNum}) by plotting the clusters with a stratified bar chart against each feature. The distribution doesn't show pure clusters against any of them, but there are some small insights:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{titleType}: Clusters 0, 2 and 7 show a relevant proportion of shorts. Cluster 0 and especially Cluster 7 additionally have a relevant proportion of tvEpisodes.  Clusters 3 and 5 also show a relevant portion of tvEpisodes, but not of shorts. Clusters 4 and 1 are made up by more than 50\% movies; their centroids indeed have the highest values for \textit{logRuntime}.
    \item \textit{ratingNum}: all clusters follow a normal distribution w.r.t. to the rating.
    \item \textit{canHaveEpisodes}: most of the True records are in the biggest cluster, Cluster 6. For every cluster, they represent a small proportion (especially for 2 and 5).
    \item \textit{moreCountriesOfOrigin}: not very informative, True represent a small portion for every cluster, but this is especially true for Cluster 2, which is almost entirely False.
    \item \textit{awardsAndNominations}: Cluster 2 is characterized by being almost entirely made of records with no awards nor nominations.
    \item \textit{hasVideos}: Cluster 2 is entirely False, whereas Cluster 3 has the most number of True out of any other cluster, showing a significantly higher proportion.
\end{itemize}
A clear characterization emerges for Cluster 2: it mostly contains shorts (its centroid has the lowest value for \textit{logRuntime}), and it has very little to no True values for every boolean variable.
Since it's greatly made up of shorts, then movies and tvEpisodes, we can expect its very low number of True for \textit{canHaveEpisodes}. Its titles are produced in just one country, they have mostly no awards nor nominations (we could expect movies and TV series to gain more accolades) and also almost no video content on their page (again, this could reasonably be a feature for blockbusters and mainstream TV series).

Subsequently, we investigated the clusters' composition w.r.t. numeric variables, first using a pair plot to gain a global view (which confirmed that the clusters are mainly separated on the basis of \textit{numGenres}) and then using appropriate plots to investigate single variables.
The stratified barchart in Figure \ref{fig:numGenres_kmeans2.png} shows how most clusters are pure w.r.t. \textit{numGenres}: 0 and 1 only have 2 genres, 3 and 7 only have 3 genres, while 4 and 6 only have 1 genre. There remains Cluster 2, which mostly has 2 genres with a small proportion of 1 genre, and Cluster 5, with around 60\% 3 genres and 40\% 2 genres.

The box plot in Figure \ref{fig:criticReviewsRatio_kmeans2.png} shows the cluster's distribution w.r.t \textit{criticReviewsRatio}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Cluster 5 is concentrated at 1.0: the reviews are mostly by critics.
    \item Cluster 4 also has a high ratio (median at around 82\%, most of the records go from 50\% to 100\%).
    \item Clusters 1 and 3 show variability, but they are a mostly balanced mix of critic and user reviews. They could represent mainstream content.
    \item Clusters 0, 2, 6 and 7 are skewed towards 0: their reviews are mostly made by the public. They could represent niche or user driven content (indie or fan-made media).
\end{itemize}

The box plots for \textit{runtimeMinutes} give the following insights:
\begin{itemize}
    \item All IQRs span from around 15 to 100 minutes.
    \item Cluster 2 has the lowest median runtime (which is coherent with what we've already discussed), followed by Clusters 0, 5 and 7, which have a median at around 40 minutes – they did in fact contain a large portion of shorts and TV episodes. Cluster 3, with a median of 75 minutes, has been characterized as probably containing mainstream content (balanced \textit{criticReviewsRatio,}, highest number oh \textit{hasVideos}): it has a mostly balanced mix of TV episodes and movies.
    \item Clusters 1 and 4 have a small IQR and a median around 90 minutes, which relates to their records being mostly movies.
    \item Cluster 6 has outliers that go to over 1000 minutes; it seems that the largest group is also the most heterogeneous. It can be linked to its significant portion of TV series, the runtime of which has been variably identified as the total runtime of the series, or the runtime per episode.    
\end{itemize}
With regards to \textit{numVotes}, the box plots for the different clusters don't add much information to the centroids' visualization: Clusters 0 and 6 have lower medians, but many upper outliers; Cluster 7, followed by Cluster 3, have the highest medians, but their large whiskers indicate that the data is very spread out.
